FOR INDIVIDUALS

See your own footprint

Review public cross-links, photo context, old institutional bios, posting times and friends' tags. Request corrections or removal where possible.

FOR PUBLISHERS & PLATFORMS

Reduce unnecessary linkability

Offer granular consent, limit search indexing of sensitive records, strip document metadata, stop automatic identity joins and make removal durable.

FOR AI PROVIDERS

Judge the whole trajectory

Detect when many benign-looking operations accumulate into a sensitive personal profile; assess purpose, consent and cumulative exposure—not only individual prompts.

10 / QUESTIONS FOR A JOURNALIST

The questions worth
asking next.

A short briefing desk for a conversation about AI, privacy, harassment and how to study the issue responsibly.

Is public information fair game?

Public accessibility is not the same as consent to unlimited repurposing. Our threat model uses contextual integrity: the original audience, purpose and information flow still matter.

Does this require a powerful hacker?

No account compromise is assumed. The evaluated system used ordinary public sources, web tools and agent reasoning. But accuracy is model-, task- and data-dependent.

Can an inference be wrong?

Absolutely. A commute, lifestyle, family relationship or wealth estimate should not be treated as a confirmed fact. The paper uses identity checks and verification, but inferred and especially AMI claims can remain uncertain.

Why might ordinary defenses fail?

A system can inspect a single request and see only harmless retrieval or extraction. The risk appears when many steps are combined. The study tested one prompt-based defense across recorded subtasks; broader guarantees remain open.

What would a responsible BBC experiment look like?

Consent from participants, synthetic or opt-in profiles, clear limits on sensitive data, no disclosure of live location or contact details, human fact-checking, an explicit deletion policy and review of what is safe to publish.

What is the single best takeaway?

The dangerous thing may not be what you posted. It may be the new conclusion someone can draw by bringing your posts together.
